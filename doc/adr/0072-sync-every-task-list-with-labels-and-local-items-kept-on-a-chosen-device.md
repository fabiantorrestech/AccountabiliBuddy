# 72. Sync every task list, with labels, and keep local items on a chosen device

Date: 2026-11-06

## Status

Accepted

## Context

ADR 48 and ADR 50 share four lists between the user's devices: the next session's tasks, Come back to (with parked thoughts), Later and the task categories. The Tasks page on a PC shows three more, and those never leave the PC that made them: the tasks past sessions left open ("Unfinished from past sessions", the last two weeks of the PC's 90-day task history), Auto-saved (tomorrow's task from a shutdown card that closed by itself, kept 30 days) and the Archive (the Notebook: 90 days of past tasks and come-backs, plus Recently deleted). The owner noticed that tasks left open on the work PC never reach the phone or the laptop, and took it for a sync fault. The code confirmed it is the design: `SyncCoordinator` writes only `nextTasks`, `parked`, `later` and `categories`. Each PC already sends its last 14 days of sessions with their checklists (ADR 24), but no device builds a list from them, and nothing tells the PC when another device has dealt with one of its old tasks.

The owner wants one app that holds every task, work and personal, and the same view on every device. That means everything syncs unless the user says otherwise for one item. The owner also wants several labels per task on top of the single category: the category is the broad bin, labels cut across categories (a project, say), and the user can filter and sort by them. Labels are plain names without colours, because the coloured category chips already crowd the Tasks page.

The owner settled four questions on 2026-11-06. A tick or a move of another PC's unfinished task, on any device, settles it everywhere, including on the PC that made it. Auto-saved stays its own list and syncs. Ninety days of task history travel, so the Archive matches on every device, while session notes and reasons stay on the PC as before. And "Local" does not just stop syncing: the user picks which device keeps the item (this one by default, or any linked device). It is carried to that device, which keeps the newest version, and it disappears everywhere else, including the device the user acted on. This holds for past-session tasks too. Turning the item back to Synced makes it reappear everywhere.

The constraints come from the existing design. Sync files are sealed per device (ADR 22), and a receiver can only read what a sender wrote. Shared lists merge item by item: newest edit wins, tombstones without text are kept 30 days, and stamps more than 2 minutes ahead are clamped (`SharedListMerge`). Older apps ignore fields they don't know, so new fields must be optional, and the format stays 1. A running session's checklist belongs to the PC that runs it (ADR 48), and nothing here may loosen a session (ADR 25).

## Decision

We will extend the sealed sync file with five optional parts and two optional item fields, all merged the way ADR 48 merges its lists, and all governed by the device's "Sync task lists" switch (ADR 71).

**Task history.** Each PC writes `taskHistory`: its own 90-day record of session checklists, including the "done outside a session" records, one entry per session start. Each task carries its id, title, steps, done time, category and colour, estimate, note and labels. Session notes and the reasons typed in a session never travel. Task notes travel under the same rule as ADR 50's notes: sealed, to the user's own devices only, never to the watch. The history is never in a QR file and is never sent on to the watch. Receivers keep each PC's history sealed, prune it at 90 days, and never count it as focus time or in stats. From it they show "Unfinished from <PC>" for the last two weeks, and an Archive that covers every device.

**Settling past tasks.** The PC's private list of past tasks moved off their session (`session.movedToLater`) becomes a shared list, `pastSettled`. Each record names a past task's id and its state: moved, done, open or deleted. A device that moves another PC's past task to Later, Today or Come back to copies it into that shared list and writes a moved record. A tick writes done, an untick writes open, and a delete writes deleted. Every device hides settled tasks from Unfinished. The PC that owns the history applies done, open and deleted to its own record (a deleted one through its usual path into Recently deleted). This needs no new sync actions, so it works between PCs as well as between a phone and a PC.

**Auto-saved.** The Auto-saved list becomes a shared list, `autoSaved`, with the same 30-day life counted from when each item was saved. Existing items are stamped once when the app updates.

**Labels.** Tasks in every list, and in the history, may carry `labels`: plain names, compared case-insensitively, shown as the first spelling seen. They travel through every move between lists. The list of labels is whatever the items carry. A rename or delete of a label is a stamped edit of each item that has it, like a category rename. As with categories (ADR 62), a copy that kept its labels wins an equal-stamp tie against one that lost them. In the title, `##word` adds the label "word" and is taken out of the text; a single `#` stays text. In the current UI the menu entry that sets a task's category, now "Label", is renamed "Category".

**Local items.** A shared item may carry `localTo`, the id of the device that keeps it. Every device writes a local item as a stub: a tombstone (`deletedAt`) with `localTo` and no text. Older apps therefore drop it as deleted, and new apps know it is local rather than gone. The keeper alone stores the full item, and it writes the stub for every local item in every file it writes, so the stub never ages out while the item is local. When the user keeps an item on another device, their device drops its own copy at once and carries the item, sealed, in a `handovers` part addressed to the keeper. The keeper writes a receipt for each handover it took in (`handoverAcks`: the item's id and edit time, kept 30 days), and the sender stops carrying it once that receipt shows. If the keeper hasn't taken the item in 30 days, it comes back to the sender, local there, with a notice. A past-session task can be kept on any device the same way. The owning PC writes a stub in place of the task in its history, and the keeper holds it in a small sealed store of kept past tasks, shown as "from <PC>". Turning an item back to Synced is a newer full edit with `localTo` cleared, made on the keeper. For a past task this also writes an open record and hands the task back to its PC. Items in a running session's checklist cannot be local.

## Consequences

Every task list now reads the same on every device, so "where did my task go" becomes a question of sync delay, not of design. Another device can settle a PC's old tasks without that PC being on. Labels give a second way to find tasks across categories, and search on the phone reaches 90 days of history.

The sync file grows, by up to a few hundred kilobytes with a full 90 days of history. It is written only when its content changes (the write gate's hash) and never in QR files. If size becomes a problem, the first fix is to send steps and notes only for the last 14 days of history. Local items add a state that older apps cannot show. A 0.1.77 phone sees a local item as deleted, which is the safe reading, but it drops labels when it edits an item, so the PCs and the phone should be updated together. A handover depends on the keeper syncing within 30 days. Until it does, the item is visible on no device except as an entry in its sender's outbox. The rename of "Label" to "Category" in the menu changes a label the owner already knows, and the Tasks page redesign (planned separately) will revisit where labels and categories sit. `doc/hard-rules.md` records the new fields, what each carries, and that session notes and reasons still never leave the PC.

Amendment, 2026-11-14 (owner, AR1b review): the PC that owns a past task keeps sending it, marked deleted with its delete time, in an optional `deleted` list of its task history entry for 30 days after the delete (the same window as the other deletion tombstones), so every device, a third one and the phone's Archive included, can list it in Recently deleted and Restore it (a newer settle record). After 30 days it drops. An older app ignores the field and so does not list it (it never shows as a live task).
