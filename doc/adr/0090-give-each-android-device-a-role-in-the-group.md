# 90. Give each Android device a role in the group

Date: 2026-11-15

## Context

- Every Android device writes `SyncDeviceCard.Kind = phone`, tablets included. The PC and the sync code take "a phone" to be any such device: the PC's steps card, wake alarm, wake estimate and smart bedtime read the newest phone file; the Health Connect and Pixel Watch tiles show when any phone is in the group; each phone with the switch on reads steps and writes goal entries.
- With a second Android device (a tablet, a work phone) these readings come from the wrong device or from two devices. The owner wants one device to count as "the phone" for group-level readings, and the others to be labelled as what they are.
- Guards (essentials, LockMyPhone-Extended holds, the work card, placement, pickups) are per-device switches today. A role that changed them, or that a guard read at run time, would be an escape hatch: relabelling the phone the user really uses as "tablet", from any device including the PC, could turn its unset guards off at once.
- The PC's alarm, bedtime reminders and wake estimate are Locked (Sleep) rows under ADR 61: making them come from another device can loosen them, so a change of main must follow the looser-change rule.
- Plan: `sr1-dr1-plan.md` and `sr1-dr1-plan-check.md` (B2, B3, B4, B9, S5, S6).

## Decision

**Each Android device has a role: main phone, tablet or other device.** The roles are one synced list, `deviceRoles`, an optional part of the sealed sync file appended after `tagScopes`: `[{"id","role","at","by"}]` in that key order, null-omitted when empty. The entry holds a device id, a role word (`main`, `tablet`, `other`), a time and the device that set it, nothing else. Every device, the PC included, carries the whole merged list; it stays in a QR file, so a QR-only phone learns its role from the PC. The copy a receiver keeps of a file may hold it. Under ADR 61 it is a **Fixed** row, like `Device.Kind`. `SyncDeviceCard.Kind` stays `phone` for every Android device.

**Merge (`SyncDeviceRoles.Merge`).** One entry per id. The newest `at` wins; a tie goes to the higher `by` (ordinal text of the D form), then to the higher role word (ordinal); times compare as instants, never by offset. An **incoming** entry whose `at` is more than 2 minutes past the receiver's trusted now is **ignored** until the receiver's own time catches up (the rule `GuidanceRules.Receive` follows, which filters only the received value); an entry this device already holds is never dropped, so a trusted clock that steps back loses nothing (SR1-S1 verify V-S1). It is never stored capped, so a stored stamp never moves forward on a later merge and a device with a fast clock cannot make every receiver rewrite and re-upload its list on each pass (SR1-S1 review S3). Stamps come from `TrustedClock`. A missing part or entry never deletes anything. Entries of removed devices (ADR 54) are left out on the way in and the way out; so is an entry with no time or no writer, or whose role word is not 1 to 32 letters and hyphens (the part carries ids, words and times, never text). The result is in id order, so every device writes the same bytes. An unknown role word is kept and written back unchanged, and it behaves as `other`. The list is read leniently: a damaged entry is skipped. The role word `none` means "no role" and resolves exactly as no entry (Unassigned with no main in the group, Other with one); it exists only so that a Cancel can put a device back to having no entry, because an entry can only be replaced by a newer one, never removed (V-S2; decided before any release, since an older app would read `none` as other).

**One main phone, by construction.** Setting X as main also writes every other `main` entry as `other` with the same stamp and writer (`SyncDeviceRoles.With`). Cancelling a change is `SyncDeviceRoles.Restore(held, previous, at, by)`, which writes again every entry the change touched with the role it had before (`none` for a device that had none), all past the entries they replace: a change of main touches the new main, the main it demoted and the new main's own earlier role, so one `With` call cannot undo it. The stamp is the caller's trusted now, or one tick past the newest entry it replaces when that is later (as `SharedListMerge.NextStamp` does), so a change made just after a faster clock's edit still wins. Two devices that set a main at the same moment can still leave two `main` entries after the merge, so the resolver decides (`SyncDeviceRoles.Effective`, for the active Android devices only): the *winning main* is the newest-stamped `main` entry of an active Android device (a tie: the higher `by`, then the higher id, ordinal D text); every other `main` entry resolves as **Other**. A device with an entry takes its role; a device with none is **Other** when a winning main exists, else **Unassigned**. **Unassigned is today's behaviour**: with no entries, or with a single Android device, nothing changes anywhere. Older apps ignore the part and treat every Android device as "the phone".

**What a role decides.** Which Android device counts as "the phone" for group-level readings: steps and sleep goal values (only a Main or Unassigned device reads Health Connect for them; a tablet or other device shows "Steps are counted on <main phone name>" and asks nothing), the PC's Health Connect and Pixel Watch tiles, `HasLinkedPhone`, and the PC's wake alarm, wake estimate, smart bedtime and `PhoneSteps`. These all read `SyncDeviceRoles.ThePhones(Effective(...))`: **the main phone when one is set, else every Unassigned device** (and among several, the newest phone file as today). Tablets and other devices are never among them, so with no main, marking an Unassigned phone a tablet or other moves these inputs. A group with only tablets or other devices counts no steps; the Steps section and the PC's detail line say "Set a main phone to count steps." It decides labels and icons (Main phone, Tablet, Other device; "Phone" while unassigned).

**What a role never decides: any guard, at run time.** No guard preference, enforcement path or session lock reads a role (a source scan checks `essentials/`, `pause/`, `placement/`, `nudge/`, `blocks/` and `guidance/`). Unchanged on purpose, by device kind for every Android device: phone sessions (ADR 85) and `GroupSessions`, placement and pickups, work-ended (ADR 67), `QrSyncService.EveryPhoneHasLatest`, device removal, goal history knowledge, the watch link, task-reminder "Rings on", the R7.5 tablet layout (screen width).

**Guards start off on a tablet or other device, on the setup path only.** Owner: "guards CAN stay on every device, but do not by default (since dependencies will differ across devices) -- that comes down to the local setup on every device", and, round 2, "on a tablet or other device, ALL phone guards start OFF (fresh setup only): essentials, LMP holds, work card, placement/pickups, and the phone guards PB already quiets. Each device turns any of them on in its own setup. Sessions started ON the tablet still use their session profile's LMP/Curbox choices."
- Before roles ship, an upgraded install writes every guard preference that is still unset with the value it has today (one migration, a marker preference). After that no code default can flip an existing device.
- The fresh-install setup assistant asks "What is this device?" before its guard steps. Tablet or Other starts those steps unticked, and every choice is written explicitly. Skipping writes the shown default; a fresh install's guard steps can already be skipped (hard rule "Works alone").
- The main phone keeps today's defaults. Nothing about guards syncs between devices.
- Owner round 2, N3: existing tablets and other devices get one card offering "Turn guards off" (through the usual looser-change wait, never during a session, never automatic). N4: a device that becomes the main phone shows a one-time "Set up guards on this phone?" card (stricter, so at once if accepted); becoming main turns nothing on by itself.

**Every role change waits for Google Drive; a change of the phones (the devices behind the PC's sleep inputs) also waits the looser-change delay.**
- **The gate (owner, both modes, applies to EVERY role change, review S1):** "a role change applies only once the sync change has been confirmed uploaded to Google Drive". Tablet to Other is a role change, so it is gated too (the row just shows "Waiting to reach Google Drive..." a little longer); only the delay below is limited to the main.
- **The gate reads this device's OWN send log, never other devices' receipts** (review BLOCKER-2). ADR 86 receipts are other devices' reports about files they read: they need a peer that reads by that method and a round trip, and never open when no peer reads by Drive. The PC's `SyncSendLog` and the phone's `SyncSendLog` already record, per method, the write time of each of this device's own puts that reached it. `SyncDeviceRoleGate.Evaluate(changeAt, newestPutByMethod, methodsInUse)` (Core, with a Kotlin twin) decides: with Drive in use it is **open** when this device's put on Drive holds a file written at or after the change (`WrittenAt >= at`, compared as instants); **waiting** otherwise. Receipts may still feed the row ("Phone 1 has it"), never the gate.
- **No Drive method (owner: "a device with no Drive method follows the methods it does have"):** a put on the shared folder or the relay counts instead (any of the methods in use). With none of those in use (QR-only, file import only, no transport) there is nothing to confirm (`NothingToConfirm`), so the change waits only for the delay and for no session ("Works alone"; it affects only that device).
- **The row:** "Waiting to reach Google Drive..." (gate waiting) then "On Google Drive · applies at 4:05 PM" (12-hour time) then applied. In ProductaBuddy mode a change of main applies at once between sessions once the gate is open; in AccountabiliBuddy mode the 10-minute delay (`TrustedClock`, countdown, Cancel, no "apply now") counts from the moment the gate opened. A change that does not change the phones (see below: Tablet to Other with a main set, a role on a device outside `ThePhones`) applies as soon as the gate is open.
- **Sent at once, applied later.** The entry is written with its REQUEST time as `at` and sent at once, so the gate has a file to confirm; this device keeps using the previous roles until the gate is open (and the delay has passed). A receiver applies after its own delay counted from the first time it saw the change (below), held during a session (ADR 68), and does not wait for this device's Drive upload: it already has the change, which is what the gate protects. One consequence the owner should know: a receiver can apply before the writer if the writer's Drive upload is slow.
- **Cancel after the change was sent.** Cancel writes `SyncDeviceRoles.Restore(held, previous, at, by)` with `previous` the roles before the change (so a device that had no entry gets `none`, and a tablet that was made main comes back a tablet) and sends that; the stamps are past the entries they replace, so it wins everywhere. Receivers drop their pending change when the phones return to the ones they applied; the writer drops its own pending state. Cancel is available until the change is applied; once applied it is an ordinary new change, with its own gate and delay.
- **Which changes get the delay (review SHOULD-4, verify V-B1).** The delay is decided on **the devices that feed the PC's sleep inputs**, never on an edit and not only on the main's id: `SyncDeviceRoles.PhonesChanged(Effective(applied), Effective(now))` compares `ThePhones` before and after and is true after a role edit, a received list, an ADR 54 removal (an older `main` entry may revive, or the group may fall to none and the phones become every Unassigned device), or a device that left. With no main, marking an Unassigned phone a tablet moves the inputs without moving a main, and a single phone made Other leaves the PC with no phone, so both wait. A device that was not in the applied list and only joins is not a change, and Tablet to Other with a main set is not one. A change of `ThePhones` is looser for the PC's Locked (Sleep) rows, so it waits the delay and is never applied during a session. `MainChanged` (the main's id only) stays as a helper. A derived change (a removal, a device leaving) has no new entry to upload, so it has no gate: the same delay only.
- **Detection and timing (review SHOULD-3, for the PC and phone tasks).** Detect a change by comparing the effective roles (`Effective(applied)` against `Effective(merged)`), never by comparing entries or stamps. Count the delay from the first time the change was seen on this device (a device-local timestamp kept with the pending state), not from the entry's `at` each time it is read.
- Refusals and holds: refused while a session the user started is seen by the editing device (PC: the Settings lock, phone sessions included; phone: the `SYNC` lock area of the PS2c seam), a change that is due in a session applies after it, no "apply now". Owner, round 2 (N2): a change of main in ProductaBuddy mode applies at once between sessions; in AccountabiliBuddy mode it always waits the looser-change delay (10 minutes).


**Settings.** Roles are set in Settings › Devices & sync, on the PC and on the phone (a row menu "Role ›"), and in the migration assistant (`devices.roles`, relevant with two or more active Android devices). A winning main that has not synced in 7 days is noted on the Devices page ("<name> is the main phone but hasn't synced in 7 days"): a reinstalled main phone gets a new id, and the old id's `main` stays the winning main until that ghost is removed.

## Consequences

- One synced list that several consumers read is costly to reverse: the shape, the merge and the resolver are shared by the PC and the phone (parity fixtures `dr1-*`). Reversing means dropping the part and the role-aware consumers; older apps already ignore it.
- A group that sets no roles behaves exactly as before. A group that sets them stops double-counting steps and sleep, and the PC's sleep inputs come from one phone.
- Because a guard never reads the role, setting a role can never loosen a guard; the cost is that a tablet's guards must be set on the tablet.
- Removing the winning main (ADR 54) can revive an older `main` entry, or leave the group with none, because `With` demotes only on the writer's own change. The plan check's S5 normalisation is not done and would not remove the need for the delay (the group would still fall to none); the effective-main comparison above makes every such change wait.
- A role change takes at least this device's own Drive upload (when it uses Drive) and, for a change of main in AccountabiliBuddy mode, 10 more minutes. That is deliberate: it is the looser-change rule applied to the PC's sleep guards, and the owner's gate on every role change.
- `BlockRef.Phone` (all phones' day-plan Curbox blocks share one ref, so a tablet's block with the same id also matches) stays out of scope; flagged.
